## 1. Spanish

### What the sources say

- History of the contact layers in Tetouan (S1, "The history of Tetouan" section): "One initial form was the Spanish variety hakitia, spoken by the Jews expelled from the Peninsula in 1492"; "The Spanish language was also introduced via Spanish-speaking moriscos arriving in small groups in the 16th" [century]; "during the years of the Spanish Protectorate in Morocco (1912-1956), Tetouan was the administrative capital" and Spanish "became the administration language in the zone"; the page adds that the Spanish bureaucracy brought "a considerable growth in the Spanish-speaking population thanks to immigration from southern Andalusia". URL: https://corvam.unizar.es/en/localities/tetouan/
- Status today (S1): "Spanish, though clearly declining as opposed to French, is currently quite conspicuous in Tetouan" and "Spanish loanwords are a sign of identity of its peculiar Arabic variety." Lexicon section: "Another feature to highlight is the presence of numerous Spanish loanwords." It calls this the most obvious difference between Tetouan Arabic and the varieties of the centre and south. The page itself gives only two labelled Spanish loans in its transcription (buskūču, paiya) — it does not print a loanword list.
- Wikipedia "Tétouan" (S2): "Many people in the city still speak Spanish."; road signs often in Spanish and Arabic "while many other signs use Arabic and French"; Spanish and French are widely used "especially among professionals and intellectual elites"; "The Spanish influence on Tetouan is very much still alive today."; Spanish state schools and an Instituto Cervantes centre exist in the city. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9touan
- Decline, national figures (S8): Spanish is spoken "particularly in the northern regions around Tetouan and Tangier"; a 2005 CIDOB survey: "21.9% of respondents from Morocco claimed to speak Spanish", falling to about 4.5% by 2017; Cervantes: "there were 11,409 students learning Spanish in Morocco in 2016" (from c. 50,000 in 2005); Spanish "has fallen well behind French and English". The page has no loanword examples. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_Morocco
- Decline, Cervantes report (S16): Ethnologue categories quoted in the report list "lenguas en desplazamiento (español; judeo-árabe marroquí)"; "El norte del país, por razones históricas y de proximidad con España, es el que muestra una presencia más viva del español."; the double diglossia is consolidating "en detrimento del francés, en el conjunto de Marruecos, y del español, en la región norte del país"; demographic cause: "unas generaciones de marroquíes que tuvieron la oportunidad de aprender español de su convivencia con españoles en el segundo tercio del siglo XX" are ageing and "no han sido reemplazados por nuevas generaciones de hispanohablantes". URL: https://observatoriodelespanol.cervantes.org/documentos/IOGE-001-El-espanol-en-el-panorama-idiomatico-marroqui-IOGE012025.pdf
- Linguistic landscape of Tetouan, 2017 fieldwork (S15): Spanish survives on shop signs; "se conserve tradicionalmente el castellano como segunda lengua, pese a que a las generaciones más jóvenes comienzan a dejarlo de lado" (in favour of French); conclusion: "la presencia del español en el paisaje lingüístico de la ciudad sigue en pleno vigor"; owners say Spanish signs give "solera" and prestige as old, genuine Tetouan businesses; "la lengua española ayudó a forjar la identidad de las generaciones tetuaníes de más edad". URL: https://quovadisromania.univie.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/QVR-57-58-Chabot-Houi-Pardos.pdf
- Code-switching: no opened source documents Darija–Spanish code-switching inside Tetouan. S21 describes code-switching for Ceuta only ("árabe ceutí; una alternancia de códigos del árabe marroquí y el español"). S1's transcription contains one Spanish phrase used as a place-name, la pueɹta ɹeina (see table).
- Old loan layer (S17): Moscoso's 473-word glossary from De la Torre's Vocabulista (1805) and Lerchundi's Vocabulario (1892); "La mayor parte de estas voces de origen castellano o español fueron recogidas en Tánger, pero también, en menor medida, en otras ciudades como Tetuán"; sources of the loans: "el español contemporáneo, el español incipiente de los siglos xv a xvii, la lingua franca, la haquitía, el castellano y el árabe andalusí"; 72 of the 473 are from lingua franca. (The pie-chart percentages are garbled in the PDF text layer and are not reported here.) URL: https://www.redalyc.org/journal/602/60274089002/60274089002.pdf
- Adaptation rules, as cited second-hand (S21 citing Sayahi 2011: 93–95 and Vicente 2011: 73): mid vowels /e/ /o/ raised to /i/ /u/; apocope of final vowels; Vicente's split between "préstamos adaptados o antiguos" (tuzzina, errumi) and "préstamos no digeridos más recientes" (barato, falso, gente). One Tetouan-specific remark in S21: "se sustituyen las vocales cerradas por las medias, pero corresponden a la zona del noroeste de Marruecos (Tánger, Tetuán, Ceuta)". URL: https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/60794/files/TAZ-TFG-2017-126.pdf?version=1
- Who still uses the Spanish words (S23, Tetouan-born journalist): "Many words are still widely used across generations"; "others, however, are exclusive to generations born before the eighties of the last century"; Spanish-origin words are sometimes pronounced "wrongly" even by natives with a high level of Spanish. URL: https://www.atalayar.com/en/opinion/mohamed-nouri/the-language-of-northern-morocco-or-the-luck-of-having-two-languages-in-one/20250325190000212611.html
- Spanish-language scholarship in the city (S12 abstract): "the only department of Spanish in former Spanish Morocco" is at Université Abdelmalek Saadi in Tetouan. URL: https://scholarsarchive.library.albany.edu/cas_llc_scholar/24

## 2. French

### What the sources say

- In Tetouan the pressure on Spanish comes from French: "Spanish, though clearly declining as opposed to French, is currently quite conspicuous in Tetouan" (S1). The CORVAM transcription of the 65-year-old mdini speaker contains one word footnoted as French (oɹigin) and two unlabelled European items (l-table, bulvar). URL: https://corvam.unizar.es/en/localities/tetouan/
- Signage (S2): road signs Spanish/Arabic "while many other signs use Arabic and French"; Spanish and French used "especially among professionals and intellectual elites". URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9touan
- Youth preference (S15): younger Tetouanis drop Spanish "generalmente a favor del aprendizaje del francés, primera lengua extranjera a nivel nacional"; the Pharmacie Centrale re-signing shows "el cambio … del español por el francés como lengua extranjera preferente en la ciudad"; the paper's conclusion opens with "Pese a la preferencia que se observa en otros ámbitos por el uso del francés como lengua extranjera en la ciudad de Tetuán". URL: https://quovadisromania.univie.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/QVR-57-58-Chabot-Houi-Pardos.pdf
- Administration and education nationally (S5): French "is taught universally and serves as Morocco's primary language of commerce and economics, culture, sciences and medicine"; Rouchdy: "For all practical purposes, French is used as a second language."; OIF: 36% speak French, 47% of students have French as medium of instruction; 2024 census: 57.7% can read and write French; in 2020 Morocco "ended Arabization" and French was reinstated for science and maths. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Morocco
- Cervantes report (S16): "Durante décadas, el francés ha sido la lengua de la administración, los negocios y la educación superior, especialmente para las élites."; "el francés es requisito para acceder a la mayoría de los empleos, incluso los no cualificados"; French "ha experimentado un desplazamiento hacia un estatus de lengua extranjera, si bien menos intenso que el que afecta al español"; pre-independence diglossia: "el francés era considerada la lengua de la vida pública y el árabe dariya, la lengua de las esferas orales y privadas". URL: (S16)
- Age profile nationally (S25, Sunergia 2024 data): French fluent 19% overall, 24% under 34, 14% at 35–54, 18% at 55–64 — "U-shaped curve". No Tetouan breakdown. URL: https://en.hespress.com/112190-112190.html

## 4. Jebli / rural northern Arabic and dialect levelling

### What the sources say

- Two competing varieties in the city (S1): "Nowadays, two varieties competing for social prestige are found in this city." The mdini (traditional) variety "is spoken by the families considered the older dwellers of Tétouan", survives "only amongst adults and older generations", and some of those families "were immigrants from the rural areas who arrived a few decades ago and probably levelled towards this variety". The urban variety mixes surviving mdini traits with "the features brought by the inhabitants of the neighbouring rural areas" and "This urban variety is used mostly by young people, who are leading the accommodation and levelling process." Young speakers also move "towards the more common pronunciation in other parts of Morocco". URL: https://corvam.unizar.es/en/localities/tetouan/
- Wikipedia (S2) says the city has its own citadin dialect distinct from Jebli, and (paraphrase) that Jeblia predominates because rural migrants settled during the 20th century. Wikipedia "Jebli Arabic" (S7): the Tetouan dialect is "an urban non-Hilalian dialect which is distinct from Jebli", but Jebli has been spoken in Tetouan since the 20th century because of migration; the article is flagged as single-source. URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9touan ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebli_Arabic
- Jebli influence on the city dialect (S14): Tangier and Tetouan are "two Northern Moroccan urban centres whose dialects have been strongly influenced by the Jebli varieties spoken in their surroundings"; the gender-neutral pronoun ntīna is a Jebli hallmark "also attested in some Northern Moroccan urban varieties (mainly Tangiers and Tetouan), probably owing to considerable rural-to-urban migration within this region"; in Tetouan ntīna "will likewise go unnoticed because it is the only attested form (Singer 1958: 246)". URL: https://digital.csic.es/bitstream/10261/288467/1/2022_benitez_the_jebli_speech.pdf
- Attitudes (S14): "Because of its association with rural communities, Jebli speech is stigmatized by speakers throughout Morocco."; the Jebli & Beldi sketches that build the stereotype "are set in the city of Tetouan and sponsored by local Tetouani businesses"; the glottal stop for /q/ — a mdini feature — is, per Vicente 2021 (not opened), valued differently by age: "the elderly regarding it as a prestige form and the younger generations stigmatizing it"; it is "highly stigmatized and associated with arrogance and/or with outdated traditions by Tetouani or Casablancan youngsters"; informants' stereotype of Jebli: "rudeness, backwardness or ignorance linked to ruralness".
- Levelling toward the Tetouan/Tangier norm, seen from Ghomara (S18, author a native speaker of Tetouan Arabic: "soy hablante nativa del dialecto de Tetuán"): Ghomara forms are in free variation with "la variedad de referencia, es decir, las urbanas de las ciudades de Tetuán y Tánger"; change goes toward "la variedad prestigiosa del norte donde sí que hay una distinción de persona: -t (1 sg.) y -ti (2 sg.) en Tetuán"; "Ángeles Vicente (2009) ya apunta a la emergencia de una variedad urbana en la ciudad de Tetuán"; Heath 2002's grouping (as cited) puts Tangier, Tetouan, Chefchaouen, Larache, Asilah and Jbala together as northern pre-Hilali dialects. URL: https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/124490/files/TESIS-2023-055.pdf

## 5. Modern Standard Arabic / Classical Arabic

### What the sources say

- Diglossia nationally (S5): MSA is the written/school form, Darija the spoken one, and the differences are large enough that the situation "can be considered as diglossia"; MSA is "practically foreign to Moroccan schoolchildren"; Darija shares 85–90% of its vocabulary with MSA; Classical Arabic is for literary, formal and religious use. URL: https://corvam.unizar.es/en/localities/tetouan/
- Script and signage (S15): bilingual Tetouan signs pair "árabe marroquí, árabe clásico, español o francés"; the Arabic on signs is Classical in form but reflects Darija/Spanish pronunciation (El Padrino written with [il] not [al], and with a letter for /p/ "ajena al alfabeto árabe clásico"). Sunergia (S25): Darija spoken by 100%, 94% call it mother tongue. The 2024 census (S2): 97.20% of Tetouanis Arabic native speakers.

## 6. Haketia / Judeo-Spanish of the Tetouan Jewish community

### What the sources say

- Definition and Arabic content (S3): Haketia is a variety of Spanish that "incorporates many elements of Moroccan Arabic"; about 34.5% of its lexicon is Arabic and 18.5% Hebrew; Arabic pharyngeals are kept; spoken in Tétouan, Tangier, Asilah, Larache, Chefchaouen, Ksar el-Kebir and by migrants in Casablanca, Ceuta, Melilla and Oran. It "did not develop a literary tradition"; schools (AIU) used Spanish and French. Decline: began in 1860 with the Spanish occupation of Tetouan (page paraphrase), c. 30,000 speakers in 1900, c. 1,000 today; a "slow renaissance" through musicians. Consonant elision occurs "particularly in Tétouan". URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haketia
- Tetuani Ladino (S4): a Haketia dialect of Tetouan's Jews, now classed extinct; re-Hispanisation from the 19th century, linked by Isaac Guershon to commerce "especially Tetouan and Gibraltar"; speakers adopted Castilian "s" and "j"; the 1859–60 war and 1860–62 occupation accelerated it; an association in Tetouan aimed to teach "pure Castilian"; Moisés Orfali calls the protectorate a "mortal blow" to Haketia; by the 1920s Haketia texts document a "dying language"; Oran's "Tetuani". No example words on the page. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetuani_Ladino
- The community (S2): Sephardim arrived after the Reconquista and spoke "a form of Judaeo-Spanish known as Haketia"; 1790 pogrom "started by Sultan Yazid"; mellah sacked in 1860 (16–18 synagogues); AIU school 1862; by 1967 "only twelve remained"; 2014 "no more than one dozen people"; Rabbi Isaac Bengualid Synagogue is now a museum. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9touan
- Haketia as a channel of Spanish words into Tetouan Arabic (S1): "One initial form was the Spanish variety hakitia, spoken by the Jews expelled from the Peninsula in 1492". (S17): "Heath (1989, p. 8) señala que algunas voces antiguas procedentes del castellano o el español han entrado en árabe marroquí por medio de la haquitía"; Moscoso counts "la haquitía hablada por los judíos de Tánger y Tetuán" among the sources of his 473 words and marks haquitía comparanda "haq." (from Benoliel, "B"). URL: https://www.redalyc.org/journal/602/60274089002/60274089002.pdf
- Present-day traces (S16): the census probably excludes "los hablantes del judeoespañol marroquí o jaquetía"; "La jaquetía, considerada variedad del español, podría ser hablada por un bajo porcentaje de la comunidad sefardí"; Ethnologue lists "judeo-árabe marroquí" among "lenguas en desplazamiento". ## Cautions for the course

1. The only first-hand, Tetouan-specific, linguist-transcribed loanwords in this log are CORVAM's buskūču, paiya and la pueɹta ɹeina (Spanish), oɹigin (French) and the italicised Standard Arabic items — all from one 65-year-old medina speaker.
2. The Atalayar word lists (S23) are a Tetouan-born journalist's, in an informal spelling, with glosses for only some items; they are not a linguist's transcription.
3. The Sayahi 2011 / Vicente 2011 examples are copied from a bachelor's thesis (S21) and could not be checked against the originals; the Moscoso entries are 19th-century Tangier–Tetouan data, not modern Tetouan usage.
4. S22's examples are "région du nord", S9's are nationwide — neither is Tetouan-specific.
5. Figures for French and English are national; none of the opened sources gives a Tetouan breakdown.
